# Automation & Integrations

Most small businesses have two or three jobs that eat hours every week
and should not. Copying data between a spreadsheet and an invoicing tool.
Rebuilding the same report every Monday. Checking a supplier page for
changes. Renaming and filing the same kind of document over and over.

Each one is small. Together they are a part-time job nobody is paid for.

This service removes them - properly, as real software that runs on its
own, not as a fragile chain of clicks that breaks the first time
something changes.

## What we build

- **Scheduled jobs** - something that runs every day, week or month
  without anyone remembering to start it: pulling data, generating a
  report, sending a summary, checking a source for changes.
- **Data pipelines** - collect from an API or a set of files, clean it,
  reconcile it, and put it somewhere useful. Messy CSV and spreadsheet
  input is the normal case, not the exception.
- **API integrations** - connect two systems that do not talk to each
  other, so a record created in one appears correctly in the other.
- **Document and report generation** - turn structured data into finished
  PDFs, spreadsheets or slide decks, formatted properly, on a schedule or
  on demand.
- **Monitoring and alerts** - watch a page, a feed, a metric or a file
  drop, and tell you only when something actually needs attention.
- **Internal tools** - a small browser-based tool that does one specific
  job for your team, with no login system to maintain and nothing to
  install.

This site is itself a working example: a scheduled pipeline that
collects market data from several sources, scores it, generates content
and publishes a static site - running unattended every day, with its
entire source code public.

## What we do not offer

- **Ongoing operations or 24/7 support.** What we build is designed to
  run unattended and fail quietly rather than loudly, and you get the
  source and documentation. But this is not a managed service with an
  on-call rota.
- **Automating things that need a licence or an identity.** Anything
  requiring credentials, payment authority or acting as you legally -
  submitting filings, moving money, signing on your behalf - is out of
  scope, and any tool we build hands those steps back to a person on
  purpose.
- **Bypassing systems that do not want to be automated.** If a service
  forbids automated access in its terms, we will say so rather than build
  something that quietly breaks the rules and your account with it.
- **Large enterprise platform work.** SAP, Salesforce and similar
  ecosystems need specialists with that platform's certifications.

## Who this is for

- **Solo operators and small teams** doing repetitive digital work by
  hand because automating it never reaches the top of the list.
- **Anyone with a spreadsheet that several people update** and which
  breaks regularly - that is usually a process wearing a spreadsheet
  costume.
- **Businesses with data in two places** that has to be kept in sync
  manually.

## How it works

1. **Describe the task you keep doing.** Best framing: what you do, how
   often, and roughly how long it takes. Timing matters - it tells us
   what the automation is worth, and whether it is worth building at all.
2. **Written proposal** with what gets automated, what stays manual, how
   it runs, what happens when it fails, fixed price and timeline.
3. **Build and hand over.** You get working software, the source code,
   and plain-language documentation for what to do when something
   changes. Deployed and running, not a script emailed to you.
4. **You own it.** Full rights to the code. No subscription and no
   dependency on us to keep it running.

## An honest note on whether it is worth it

Not everything should be automated. If a task takes ten minutes a month,
automating it is a hobby, not an investment. If it takes two hours a
week, it is roughly a hundred hours a year and the arithmetic makes
itself.

We will tell you which one yours is before quoting. Talking you out of a
small job is cheaper for both of us than building something you will
resent paying for.
