# Design, Branding & Visual Identity

Most small businesses do not need a full rebrand. They need the specific
thing that is currently holding them back: a logo that works at favicon
size, a deck that does not look like it was built in a hurry, or a set of
graphics that finally match each other.

That is what this is - design work scoped to what you actually need, not a
package with items you will never use.

## What we make

- **Logos and wordmarks** - delivered as vector files that stay sharp at
  any size, plus the flattened versions you need day to day, and a plain
  favicon that is still legible at 16 pixels.
- **Visual identity** - colour palette, typography, and the rules for how
  they go together, written down so anything made later still looks like
  you. Includes light and dark variants, since half your audience will see
  the dark one.
- **Marketing graphics** - social images, ad creatives, banners, product
  mockups and cover art, in the exact dimensions each platform wants.
- **Presentation and document design** - pitch decks, one-pagers,
  proposals and reports that look considered instead of assembled.
- **Illustration and artwork** - icon sets, spot illustrations, and
  section artwork drawn to fit your identity rather than pulled from a
  stock library everyone else uses too.
- **Web and interface design** - landing pages, product pages, dashboards
  and email templates, designed against real content rather than dummy
  text.

## Who this is for

- **Businesses whose materials do not match** - a logo from one designer,
  a deck from a template, and social graphics made in a hurry. The fix is
  usually a small set of rules, not new assets.
- **Anyone launching something** where first impressions carry real weight
  and there is no in-house designer.
- **Existing clients of the digital products here** - if you commissioned
  a tailored product, having it designed to match your brand rather than
  ours is the obvious next step.

## Who this isn't for

- You need a large volume of near-identical creative every week. That is
  a production workflow, and an in-house designer or a template system
  will serve you better and cheaper.
- You want a logo in 24 hours for under fifty euros. Plenty of services do
  that; we are not one of them, and pretending otherwise would waste your
  time.
- You already have a brand guide you are happy with and just need someone
  to apply it. Worth asking anyway, but often a production freelancer is
  the better fit.

## How it works

1. **Tell us what is not working.** The specific symptom is more useful
   than a general brief - "our deck loses people on slide three" points
   somewhere real.
2. **Written proposal.** Exactly what you get, in which file formats, how
   many revision rounds, the fixed price and the timeline. Agreed before
   anything starts.
3. **Concepts, then refinement.** You see real directions early, applied
   to your actual content, not polished mockups that hide the awkward
   cases. Two revision rounds are included as standard.
4. **You own everything.** Full commercial rights, source files included -
   editable vector and layered files, not just exported images. No
   licensing terms, no ongoing fees, no permission needed to change it
   later.

## What it costs

It depends entirely on scope, so an honest figure needs a conversation
first. A standalone logo with basic usage rules sits at the lower end; a
full identity system with templates and artwork is a larger piece of work.

You get the exact number in the proposal before committing. If your budget
does not match the scope, we will say so and suggest what to cut - a
smaller piece done properly beats a full package done thinly.
