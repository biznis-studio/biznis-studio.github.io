title: 7 places actually worth reading if you freelance or run a tiny business
description: An honest, no-affiliate reading list - communities, references, and one official EU resource most sellers find too late.
---
There is no shortage of "top 50 resources for freelancers" lists. Most of
them are padding wrapped around affiliate links. This one is short, has no
affiliate links anywhere, and each entry says what the place is actually
*good for* - because most of these are only useful if you go in with the
right expectation.

## Communities where real situations get discussed

**[r/freelance](https://www.reddit.com/r/freelance/)** - the closest thing
to a public archive of every client situation you will ever hit: scope
creep, late payment, ghosting, raising rates. Go for the specific threads,
not general advice; the comment sections on concrete situations are where
the value is.

**[The Workplace Stack Exchange](https://workplace.stackexchange.com/)** -
structured Q&A on difficult work conversations, with answers that are voted
on rather than shouted. Particularly good when you need wording for an
awkward message and want to see how different phrasings land.

**[Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/)** - interviews and
running logs from people building small digital businesses, usually with
real revenue numbers attached. Useful antidote to both hype and cynicism:
you see what "moderately successful" actually looks like, which is a data
point almost nothing else publishes.

**[Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/)** - not a freelancing
community, but the comment threads on any "I quit my job to freelance" or
"my SaaS failed" post are consistently more honest than the posts
themselves. Also where a surprising share of this site's own market
research signals come from.

## References you'll want bookmarked before you need them

**[Freelancers Union](https://www.freelancersunion.org/)** - a US
nonprofit, but their contract and late-payment resources are useful
regardless of where you are based. Their model contract clauses are a good
sanity check even if you already have your own template.

**[The EU's One Stop Shop VAT portal](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/)** -
if you sell digital products to EU consumers, this is the official
explanation of how VAT registration and reporting actually works. Most
sellers discover this landscape *after* their first sale. (Practical note:
marketplaces that act as merchant of record - Gumroad among them - handle
this for you, which is a genuinely underrated reason small sellers start
there.)

**[Gumroad's creator resources](https://gumroad.com/blog)** - platform
blog, so read it knowing that, but the practical posts on pricing and
launching digital products are grounded in aggregate data from an actual
marketplace rather than one person's anecdote.

## The pattern behind the list

Notice what is *not* here: no courses, no gurus, no "one weird trick"
newsletters. Communities with voting, sources with real numbers, and
official documentation - places where being wrong gets corrected. That
filter alone removes most of the noise.
